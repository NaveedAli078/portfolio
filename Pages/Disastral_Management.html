Overview

Why Flood Risk Matters

Pakistan is one of the world's most flood-prone countries, experiencing devastating events almost every year. The 2022 super-floods inundated one-third of the country, displacing 33 million people and causing over $30 billion in economic losses. Despite these recurrent disasters, comprehensive and high-resolution flood hazard maps remain scarce.

Geospatial technology — particularly satellite remote sensing and cloud computing on Google Earth Engine — has transformed how we detect, map, and assess flood risk at both regional and national scales.

Research Topics

Areas of Investigation

🌊

Flood Susceptibility Mapping

Using ML classifiers (RF, SVM, XGBoost) with multi-source conditioning variables including DEM, slope, TWI, LULC, and rainfall.

🛰️

SAR-based Inundation Detection

Sentinel-1 SAR backscatter change detection to map flooded extents in near-real-time during active flood events.

☁️

GEE Cloud Processing

Leveraging Google Earth Engine's petabyte-scale satellite archive to perform large-area flood mapping without local computing infrastructure.

⚠️

Risk & Exposure Assessment

Combining flood hazard maps with population, infrastructure, and agricultural exposure data to produce actionable risk indices.

Methodology

Technical Approach

My research workflow combines multi-source spatial data with machine learning pipelines to produce flood susceptibility and hazard maps at 30 m resolution:

"Integrating Sentinel-1 SAR, optical imagery, DEM derivatives, and climate data within a Google Earth Engine pipeline enables robust flood susceptibility mapping at spatial scales previously impossible with desktop GIS."

Key methodological steps: (1) multi-source data preprocessing and normalization, (2) conditioning variable extraction — slope, aspect, TWI, NDVI, drainage density, (3) ML classifier training with field-validated flood inventory, (4) spatial cross-validation, (5) final susceptibility map generation.
