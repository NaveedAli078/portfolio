Overview

Cities Growing Faster Than Plans

Urbanization is one of the most profound land-use transformations of the 21st century. In Pakistan, cities like Rawalpindi, Lahore, and Karachi are expanding at accelerating rates — converting agricultural land, wetlands, and green spaces into impervious built-up surfaces.

This unplanned sprawl creates cascading pressures on water availability, urban heat islands, green infrastructure, and ecosystem carbon storage. Understanding where, how fast, and at what cost cities are expanding is critical for sustainable land governance.

Research Topics

Areas of Investigation

🏙️

LULC Change Detection

Multi-temporal Landsat and Sentinel-2 classification to quantify land cover transitions from 1990 to present across major Pakistani cities.

🌡️

Urban Heat Island (UHI)

Land Surface Temperature (LST) retrieval from Landsat thermal bands to map urban heat intensity and identify cooling deficit areas.

🌳

Green Space Loss

Tracking the conversion of vegetation and agricultural land to impervious surfaces using NDVI time-series and change-vector analysis.

💧

Hydrological Impact

Assessing how increasing impervious cover affects surface runoff, groundwater recharge, and flood risk in peri-urban areas.

Methodology

Technical Approach

Urban growth analysis leverages the 40+ year Landsat archive available on Google Earth Engine. Using supervised classification (Random Forest, SVM) with ground-truth training samples, multi-class LULC maps are produced at 5-year intervals. Post-classification change detection matrices quantify class-to-class transitions.

"Satellite-based LULC change detection at decadal intervals provides the most spatially comprehensive and cost-effective method for tracking urban expansion, particularly in data-sparse environments like Pakistan."
